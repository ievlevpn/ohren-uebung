# Sources

Third-party scores are downloaded into `prep/sources/` (git-ignored). Patching is done in code, not by hand.

## Mutopia (LilyPond) — `fugue_prep/lily.py`

`fetch_mutopia` downloads the `.ly`; `render_voices` patches a copy (in `sources/mutopia/voices/`) and runs `lilypond`:

- adds `\midi { \context { \Score midiChannelMapping = #'voice } }` (replacing any existing `\midi` block; an old-syntax `\tempo` inside it is dropped);
- removes every `\change Staff = ...` (a voice that crosses staves otherwise gets a second MIDI channel on the other staff);
- no extra `Voice` contexts were needed for BWV 988 Var. 3 and 15: the two upper voices are `<< \a \\ \b >>`, already separate voices.

Repeats are not unfolded: each section sounds once. Voices come out top first (highest mean pitch).

Multi-file Mutopia sources (no `<name>.ly`, e.g. `BachJS/BWV1080/contrapunctusI`) come as `<name>-lys.zip`: `fetch_mutopia` unpacks it into `sources/mutopia/<name>-lys/` and `render_voices` runs LilyPond with `-I` on that folder so the `\include`d parts are found. Old scores without explicit `Voice` contexts (e.g. `contrapunctusVI`) put every staff on MIDI channel 0 of its own track; `read_midi` takes a voice to be a (track, channel) pair.

Used: BWV 988 Var. 3, 6 (`beats_per_quarter: 2`, beats are eighths), 9, 12, 15, 18 (`beats_per_quarter: 0.5`, beats are half notes), 21, 24 and 27 (`beats_per_quarter: 2`); BWV 1080 Contrapunctus I and VI (the rest of BWV 1080 comes from kern, below). Var. 12, 24 and 27 swap the leading voice (at the double bar; in Var. 24 every eight bars), so their markings give the canon as `span`ned sections.

## Musical Offering canons — `transcriptions/bwv1079-*.yaml`

No encoding of the riddle canons exists on Mutopia or KernScores (down); Nate Eldredge's LilyPond crab canon (github.com/captbaritone/crab_canon) agrees note for note with ours. The written voices (Thema and dux) come from Reccmo's MIDI files on IMSLP (BWV 1079 page, #221462–#221466) via `scripts/musical_offering.py` (one-off, not part of the build; the MIDI files must be downloaded by hand), checked against the Bach-Gesellschaft edition vol. 31.2, IMSLP #10042 (`sources/imslp/IMSLP10042-BWV1079-b.pdf`, git-ignored; PDF page = printed page): riddles on pages 8–9, Kirnberger's resolutions on pages 41–43. Followers are computed by the rule; where the resolution inflects a 6th or 7th differently from the strict mirror, the markings carry `exceptions`. Endings of the perpetual canons (`end`/`coda`) follow Reccmo and are editorial.

The other canons come the same way from Reccmo's MIDI files #221461 (BWV 1079/2), #221463 (3b), #221467 (4), #221470 (6) and #221475 (9), kept in `sources/imslp/midi/` (git-ignored; open #221475 with `clip=True`, it has a malformed sysex), via `scripts/musical_offering_rest.py`. Reccmo's spelled-out trills become their main note and the tied notes he strikes again become one note, each checked on the plate. Note-ons and note-offs pair first on, first off per channel and pitch: the Fuga canonica's harpsichord track holds both hands, which sound the same c' or b♭ at once in bars 16–17 and 51–53; there each hand keeps its note, with the durations printed on pp. 43–44. Page references (IMSLP10042): Canon perpetuus super thema regium, riddle p. 8, Kirnberger's resolution p. 41; Canon a 2 violini in unisono, riddle p. 8, resolution p. 41; Fuga canonica in Epidiapente, riddle pp. 10–11, resolution pp. 43–44 (Reccmo's harpsichord track holds both hands: the dux is the notes the flute answers, a fifth higher 40 beats later; from beat 271 the upper line); Canon a 2 quaerendo invenietis, riddle p. 20, BGA's solution A p. 49 (after the Allgemeine Musikalische Zeitung VIII, p. 287; Reccmo plays solutions a and b, only a is used); Canon perpetuus (BWV 1079/9), printed in score pp. 36–37, so all three voices are transcribed (`score: true`) and the violin is matched, not computed: its mirror drops a minor seventh for the flute's notes from bar 18 beat 4 (answered from bar 20) to the repeat. Title "Canone perpetuo" as the BGA heads it. The Canon a 4 (BWV 1079/7; riddle p. 20, BGA resolution pp. 50–51) has no MIDI among Reccmo's files (#221475's file name says "Canon_a4", but it holds the Canone perpetuo), so its one written line is transcribed by hand from the plate (`transcriptions/bwv1079-canon-a4.yaml`): a G clef on the bottom line and an F clef share one staff, so the line reads as g' in a treble and as G two octaves lower in a bass. The other three voices are computed as the resolution places them: bass I two octaves lower 7 bars later, treble II at the same pitch 14 bars later, bass II two octaves lower 21 bars later. Cross-check: dardel.info's MIDI of the canon (`sources/other/dardel-BWV1079_11-canon-a-4.mid`, four tracks, a harpsichord version; licence not verified, kept for personal use only, git-ignored) agrees with the computed voices note for note, its ending included (three passes, every voice cut at beat 336 and holding its note there for two beats; editorial). `tests/test_pieces.py` checks that agreement when the file is present. The two ricercars come from kern (below).

## Musical Offering, the two ricercars — `craigsapp/bach-musical-offering`

`fetch_musical_offering` (`fugue_prep/sources.py`) clones github.com/craigsapp/bach-musical-offering into `sources/bach-musical-offering/` (`kern/offering-001.krn`, Ricercar a 3; `kern/offering-002.krn`, Ricercar a 6; encoded by Craig Stuart Sapp from the Bach-Gesellschaft edition, vol. 31.2, 2024, IMSLP10042 pp. 3-7 and 12-19). Read with `read_kern`: neither file has spine splits, chords or sic/ossia readings, so no note is dropped; the a 3's last bar has two grace notes (appoggiaturas f' and b'), its only secondary notes (see grace notes below). Both are alla breve and use `beats_per_quarter: 0.5` (a beat is a half note): the a 3 has four quarters a bar (185 bars, the BGA's barring), the a 6 two whole notes a bar (103 bars; the BGA bars it in ¢ with two half notes a bar, so one kern bar is two of the plates'). The files' `*MM` values name no source; the tempi are editorial.

The subject is the royal theme to its close on the tonic c (a 3: bars 1-9, soprano; a 6: bars 1-5, alto), checked on the plates; the answer is tonal in both (G B♭ C E♭ F♯). Every statement `find_entry` finds is marked (ten in the a 3, twelve in the a 6: ten plus the two `ref: answer` ones below); the a 6's bass in bar 99 only to the F (`part: head`, `tail: free`), where it turns into the final cadence. Two statements are matched against the first answer, not the subject (`ref: answer`): the a 6's first soprano in bar 66 (the answer's shape on E♭, its third major) and second soprano in bar 86 (the answer with B♮, p. 18), each 3 semitones off the subject in one interval. Both ricercars are played on the harpsichord: the scores name no instruments, and the a 6's bass reaches B♭1 (bar 67), below the cello and organ ranges; the a 6's sixth voice takes a sixth texture, `cross` (cross-hatched).

## Inventions and Sinfonias — `humdrum-tools/inventions`

`fetch_inventions` (`fugue_prep/sources.py`) clones github.com/humdrum-tools/inventions into `sources/inventions/` (`kern/inven01-15.krn`, `kern/sinfo01-15.krn`; encodings by David Huron). Read with `read_kern`; the 15 Inventions have no spine splits, so nothing is dropped. Pieces in 3/8 and 9/8 (Inventions 3, 4, 6, 10) use `beats_per_quarter: 2` on the kern source, so a beat is an eighth. Invention 3 opens with a one-eighth upbeat, which the build keeps at beat 0: its beats run one eighth ahead of Bach's barlines. Tempi follow the recording noted in each file (András Schiff), rounded.

The 15 Sinfonias (`sinfo01-15.krn`, encoded by Craig Stuart Sapp from the Bach-Gesellschaft edition, 2024) have three voices and no spine splits, so nothing is dropped and no piece is held back. Sinfonias 2 (12/8), 6 (9/8), 11 and 13 (3/8) use `beats_per_quarter: 2`; Sinfonia 15 (9/16) uses `beat_unit: 0.75` (a new kern source option, the beat in quarter notes; onsets are divided by it): a beat is a dotted eighth, three a bar, and its countermotive starts on a third of a beat (4/3). These files name no recording, and their `*MM` values have no stated source, so the Sinfonias' tempi are editorial.

## The Art of Fugue — `craigsapp/art-of-the-fugue`

`fetch_art_of_fugue` (`fugue_prep/sources.py`) clones github.com/craigsapp/art-of-the-fugue into `sources/art-of-the-fugue/` (fetched at commit c970eb9, 2025-02-04). Every file is encoded by Craig Stuart Sapp (`!!!ENC`/`!!!EED`, 2024) from the Bach-Gesellschaft edition (`!!!SMS1`: vol. 25.1, 1878, ed. Wilhelm Rust; `!!!SMS2`: vol. 47, 1926), with page links to IMSLP #504269 and #469177. Contrapunctus I and VI stay on their Mutopia sources (above), unchanged.

**Rights.** No file has a copyright or licence record: there is no `!!!YEC`, `!!!YEM`, `!!!YER`, `!!!YEP` or `!!!YOR` in any of the 20 files, and the repository has no licence file (GitHub reports none). The records present are `COM`, `CDT`, `OPR`, `OTL`, `SCA`, `SMS*`, `PED`, `ONB`, `ENC`/`END`/`EED`/`EEV`, `URL-pdf`, `URL-youtube`, `AGN`, `RWG`, layout and filter records. The same holds for `craigsapp/bach-musical-offering` (above). Bach's notes and the 1878/1926 edition are in the public domain; the encodings themselves carry no stated licence, so their reuse terms are unstated (`tests/test_art_of_fugue.py` fails if a file gains a `!!!YE*` record).

The file numbers are not Bach's; each piece's file was chosen by its `!!!OTL` record (`tests/test_art_of_fugue.py` checks them): 002-011 Contrapunctus 2-11; 018a/018b "Contrapunctus 18 (XII), a 4. (rectus)/(inversus)" = Contrapunctus XII; 016a/016b "Contrapunctus 16 (XIII), a 3." = Contrapunctus XIII; 015 "Canon in Augmentation in Inversion"; 012 "Canon alla Ottava"; 014 "Canon alla Decima. Contrapunto alla Terza"; 013 "Canon alla Duodecima in Contrapunto alla Quinta"; 019 "Contrapunctus XIX: Quadruple Fugue on the name B-A-C-H" (`!!!ONB: Unfinished`) = Contrapunctus XIV. The files 001 and 006 (Contrapunctus 1 and 6) are not used. Catalogue numbers follow BWV 1080/1-19 (canons 14-17, Contrapunctus XIV 19), and the collection is ordered by them.

Read with `read_kern`; no file has sic/ossia readings. Secondary notes (nothing sounding is dropped): Contrapunctus 5, 12 (spine splits, bars 86-90); 7, 41 (the soprano's split, bars 56-61); 8, 2 grace notes (bar 38); 11, 6 (2 of them grace notes); grace notes in the Canon alla Ottava (15), alla Decima (5) and per Augmentationem (6). Beats: quarter notes for ¢ and C; Contrapunctus XIII opens with a quarter upbeat (`pickup: 1`, checked against each file); Contrapunctus XII in 3/2 uses `beats_per_quarter: 0.5` (half-note beats, 3 a bar); the Canon alla Ottava in 9/16 `beat_unit: 0.75` (dotted-eighth beats, 3 a bar); its repeat (bars 5-80) is not unfolded, i.e. not played. The `*MM` values name no source; all tempi are editorial. The Canon alla Decima's file notes that a G♮ in the top voice (bar 75) "is probably supposed to be g#"; it is kept: the kern writes an explicit natural (`12gn`, the BGA's), bars 40ff restate bars 1ff with the voices swapped, the chord there is C♯ E G B♭, and a G♯ would make an augmented octave with the equivalent note of bar 36. Contrapunctus XIV ends where the file (and Bach's manuscript) does, in bar 239, the tenor alone; nothing is completed.

Fugues: subjects and entries as for the WTC (find_entry scans of every voice and onset, checked by hand; forms by `wtc_form`, Contrapunctus VII by its first leap as Contrapunctus VI). Contrapuncti VIII, IX, X, XI and XIV use `fugue.subjects` (I, II, III in the order heard); only subject I's statements are told apart as subject or answer. Ornamented or varied statements are marked to where they leave the subject (`part: head`). The mirror fugues' inversus is checked against the rectus voice by voice (a diatonic mirror in D minor, voices exchanged, at least 96 % of the notes). Canons: the score's notes are matched against the rule (interval, delay, inversion, augmentation) in sections where the voices change roles.

Instruments: strings, like Contrapunctus I and VI (violin, viola on the alto, cello on the bass; tenors that go below C3 play the cello); the four canons on the harpsichord: the Canon alla Ottava's upper voice spans D2-B♭5 and the Canon per Augmentationem's lower voice reaches B1, outside every string range, and all four are two-stave keyboard pieces.

## The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I fugues — `humdrum-tools/bach-wtc-fugues`

`fetch_repo` clones github.com/humdrum-tools/bach-wtc-fugues into `sources/bach-wtc-fugues/` (`kern/wtc1fNN.krn`, encoded by David Huron from the Bach-Gesellschaft edition, vol. 14; the edition's pages are in `reference-edition/`). Each fugue voice is its own spine. The files' `*MM` tempi name no source, so the tempi are editorial, read as quarters per minute and given in beats (BWV 856 in 3/8, 860 in 6/8 and 864 in 9/8 use `beats_per_quarter: 2`, a beat is an eighth: 87 quarters = 174 eighths; BWV 867 in 2/2 uses `beats_per_quarter: 0.5`, a beat is a half note: 126 quarters = 63 halves).

No sounding note is dropped. `read_kern` keeps a chord's top note as the voice's (primary) note and its other tones as secondary notes (`c: true` in the piece JSON); where a spine splits into sub-voices, the first sub-voice continues the voice and the others become secondary notes. Secondary notes play and are drawn, but `find_entry`, canons and entry emphasis use primary notes only. The one exception is an edition's alternative reading:

- **sic/ossia pairs** (`*S/sic`, `*S/ossia`): the Bach-Gesellschaft's main text against an alternative it prints ("Oder", BWV 846 bar 9). The markings name these bars in `source.alternatives`; there the alternative is dropped (and reported as a build warning), never a sounding note: BWV 846 bars 4 (alto: ossia A4–D4 for two eighth rests), 9 (soprano: ossia E5 dotted eighth–F♯5 sixteenth for two eighths) and 12 (soprano: ossia A4–G♯4 for G♯4–A4); BWV 857 bar 41 (alto: ossia E♭4 for C4); BWV 863 bar 32 (tenor: ossia B3 for the main text's B♯3, which the edition marks sic); BWV 864 bars 51 (alto: ossia F♯4 for E4) and 53 (alto: ossia E3 for G♯3). A bar listed there without a split is an error.
- **closing splits** (`!! Four-voice material to the end`) are kept whole as secondary notes: BWV 848 bar 55 (8 notes; the E♯4 that doubles a primary note sounds once), BWV 851 bars 43–44 (12 in the soprano, plus the alto's chord tones), BWV 852 bar 37 (D♭–C–C♭–B♭), BWV 860 bars 84–85 (6 in the soprano, 1 in the bass), BWV 861 bars 33–34 (6 in the alto), BWV 869 bars 75–76 (10 in the bass).

Secondary notes added by the rebuild (chord tones and split lines; all other kern pieces have none): BWV 846 1 (the final chord's E5), 847 7, 848 8, 850 1, 851 18, 852 4, 860 7, 861 6, 865 12 (chord tones in bars 80–87), 869 10, Invention 1 3, Invention 8 2. Adding BWV 849 and 865 (the tie carried into a chord, `commented_from`, `subjects`, `head: short`) left every other piece's built JSON byte for byte unchanged (compared on all 76 pieces).

**Grace notes** (kern `q`/`qq`: appoggiaturas, acciaccaturas) sound: `read_kern` keeps each as a secondary note 1/16 of a quarter long (`kern.GRACE`, scaled with `beats_per_quarter`/`beat_unit` like every note), just before its main note (a run of n takes the n/16 of a quarter before it); the main note keeps its place and length, and grace notes are never matched (entries, canons). In the library: Sinfonia 5 (66), the Ricercar a 3 (2) and the Art of Fugue files 008 (2), 011 (2), 012 (15), 014 (5) and 015 (6); no other kern source has any. Adding them left every piece's primary notes, marks, captions and threads unchanged (compared on all 75 built pieces; `tests/test_kern.py` checks it per source).

BWV 849 (C♯ minor, five voices, 2/2, `beats_per_quarter: 0.5`) is a triple fugue. Its markings give `fugue.subjects` (ids I, II, III, each with its own voice, start and length) instead of `fugue.subject`, and every subject or answer entry names its subject (`subject: II`); entry marks are labelled "subject II", "answer III" (`fugue.subject_spec`, `first_answer(fugue, subject)`: forms are read per subject). Subject I is the bass's C♯ B♯ E D♯ (bars 1-3); subject II the first soprano's two bars of running eighths (bars 36-37, the lead-in quarters before it vary and are not part of it); subject III the alto's C♯ F♯ F♯ F♯ E♯ D♯ E♯ (bars 49-50); all three sound together from bar 49. Most statements of subject I open with a half note instead of the whole note: `head: short` (an entry option, nothing else is loosened) places notes 2..n half the first note's length earlier and still compares every interval. The glossary entry "Double and triple fugue" (aliases double fugue, triple fugue) covers the technique.

BWV 865 (A minor): `wtc1f20.krn` adds a fifth spine (`*+`) after the barline of bar 80 and comments out every line of bars 80–87 (`!!`-prefixed, "Five-voice material until the end"). The markings' `source.commented_from: 80` makes `read_kern` read them again (`kern.restore_commented`, on a copy of the text): the `!!` lines with one field per spine become data lines, and the added spine becomes a voice of its own (the "fifth voice", top), silent before bar 80: the `*+` and the lines that set it up are dropped, and every earlier line gets a field for it (header interpretations and barlines copied, a whole-bar rest on each bar's first data line; 4/4 only). 205 notes sound from bar 80 (the file's 206 onsets less the tenor's unison E in the last chord), 12 of them secondary chord tones. The fifth voice states the subject on D from bar 84, marked, in stretto with the tenor's inversion and the soprano. The file writes the end of the bass's tie (A2, held from bar 83) on the octave A3 of the last chord (`1AA; 1A;]`); `read_kern` carries a tie into the chord tone of the held pitch even when it is not the top note or when the tie's end sits on another tone, so the A2 holds to the end and the A3 is a new secondary note. Checked note for note against the Bach-Gesellschaft plate (`reference-edition/wtc1f20.pdf`, p. 73, bars 80–87). Bar 69 has a sic/ossia pair (soprano), listed in `alternatives`.

BWV 864 bar 25: the file's spine exchange (`*x`) is commented out, so the soprano's E at the downbeat and the line it goes on with sit in different spines; that statement is also varied and stays unmarked.

Entries: a statement is marked when `find_entry` finds it complete; where its first note is held over (longer, tied over from before the statement's place, or another pitch) the entry has `head: free` and `start` is the statement's place, where its last note comes late or turns elsewhere `tail: free`; either way the freed note may be late by at most the subject note's length. Statements varied inside are listed as unmarked in each markings file. A varied answer that is too far from the subject but within find_entry's tolerance of the first answer (the first entry with form answer, whole, fixed head and tail) is marked `ref: answer` and matched against that answer's notes: BWV 853 bar 64 (soprano, the inverted answer), BWV 869 bar 47 (bass, the answer on D). Subject boundaries follow the cadence (BWV 851 ends on the A of bar 3, 852 on the B♭ of bar 2, 857 on the F of bar 4, 860 on the half cadence's D of bar 4, 867 on the D♭ of bar 3 where the answer enters; the varied continuations after it are not part of the subject). Forms follow the notes from note 2 on: where the answer's body differs from the subject's (BWV 853: the third after the fourth), the better fit; otherwise the pitch level of notes 3..n — the answer's level (a fifth up) is marked `answer` (tests: `test_wtc_forms_follow_the_notes`, which fails on an undecided statement). Where the two fits tie (BWV 866 bar 26: one interval like the answer's, the next like the subject's), the pitch level decides. Stretto groups: entries that begin at least a beat before the previous entry's last note.
